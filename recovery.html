WHEN DISASTER STRIKES

What Should You Do?

During an emergency, staying calm and following reliable safety instructions can help reduce injuries and protect lives.

Remember: Safety Comes First

Every disaster is different. Follow instructions from local authorities and emergency responders. Do not put yourself in danger to save belongings or return to an unsafe area.

1. Protect Yourself

  • Follow the correct safety actions for the hazard.
  • Move away from immediate danger if it is safe to do so.
  • Stay away from damaged buildings, fallen power lines and unstable structures.
  • Keep children and vulnerable people close and supported.

2. Get Reliable Information

  • Listen to official warnings and emergency instructions.
  • Use a charged phone or battery-powered radio when available.
  • Check information before sharing it with others.
  • Do not spread unverified reports or rumours.

3. Help Others Safely

  • Contact emergency responders when someone needs urgent help.
  • Assist children, older adults and people with disabilities when safe.
  • Share essential supplies without entering dangerous areas.
  • Do not attempt rescues beyond your training or abilities.

Important Safety Reminder

Never walk or drive through floodwater, touch fallen electrical wires, or enter a damaged building before it has been declared safe. If evacuation is advised, follow the designated route and instructions from local authorities.

AFTER THE DANGER

Steps Toward Recovery

Recovery takes time. Communities can rebuild safely by addressing urgent needs first and planning for future risks.

1. Check Health and Safety

Make sure everyone is accounted for and seek medical assistance for injuries or illness. Avoid damaged structures, contaminated water and unsafe food. Follow public health advice.

2. Find Safe Shelter and Essentials

Use a safe shelter if your home is damaged or unsafe. Arrange access to clean drinking water, food, essential medicine, sanitation and necessary supplies.

3. Assess Damage Carefully

Document property damage when it is safe, and report hazards to the relevant authorities. Do not re-enter damaged buildings until they have been checked and declared safe.

4. Support Community Recovery

Work with trusted local organisations and community leaders to distribute supplies fairly, restore essential services and support people who need additional assistance.

5. Rebuild More Safely

When rebuilding, consider local hazards, safer building practices, improved drainage, accessible evacuation routes and better emergency communication to reduce future risks.
